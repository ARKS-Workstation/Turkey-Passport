01 · The show

Two friends.
One topic: AI.

ARKS Sharks is a vertical talk show from a Dubai studio. Ahmed and Kamran chat like old friends about the newest AI tools, what AI means for Dubai, and the funniest things AI has done. Every fact is sourced and shown on screen.

“We hunt the AI news… so you don't have to.”

Friendly

Two mates talking, never a lecture. Banter, laughs, karak on the table.

Smart

Real, recent AI news explained in plain words, with what it means for you.

Funny

A funny corner of real AI fails in every episode. We laugh with the tech, not at people.

Honest

Every claim has a source on screen. AI-made pictures are labelled "Illustration".

The ARKS Sharks mark

Dashed line = minimum clear space: the height of the letter "A" on every side.

Anatomy

Circuit fin. A shark fin drawn from glowing circuit-board traces: the shark (sharp, hunting for news) built out of AI.

Wave line. The surface of the water. The fin cuts through it: news breaking the surface.

Chrome wordmark. "ARKS SHARKS" in a wide geometric sans with a chrome finish and a cyan edge.

03 · Logo do's & don'ts

Keep the mark intact

✓ Do

Logo slam over the studio, shadow version.
  • Use the shadow version over video and photos.
  • Keep it centred in the upper third of a 9:16 frame.
  • Give it clear space: never touching captions, faces or widgets.
✕ Don't stretch or squash
✕ Don't recolour, never red
✕ Don't place on bright, busy video without the shadow version
✕ Don't rotate or tilt
✕ Don't separate the fin from the wordmark
✕ Don't fade or use low contrast

09 · Character rules

Always on-model

✓ Always

  • Same faces, hair, beards and outfits in every shot, using the master images as references.
  • Repeat the identity description word for word in every generation prompt (see page 19).
  • Ahmed sits left, Kamran sits right. Karak glasses on the table or in hand.
  • Podcast mics on boom arms beside them, never covering a mouth.
  • Natural, friendly acting: looking at each other while they chat, at the camera to open and close.

✕ Never

  • No outfit changes mid-scene. No ties, suits or sunglasses. (The AI once gave Kamran a tie when he said "tie": cut around it.)
  • No extra people on set, no other characters wearing the hosts' outfits.
  • No red clothing or props.
  • No realistic photo versions: always the animated style.
  • Never put words in the hosts' mouths about real people or companies without a source.

12 · 9:16 layout & safe zones

Every frame has a place for everything

Widget zone · top

y 58–625 px, 800 px wide, centred. Topic cards live here. Faces must never be covered: frame the hosts lower when widgets are used.

Faces · middle

y 660–1180 px. Keep clear of all graphics. Name tags go just below the faces on first appearance.

Captions · lower middle

Baseline at about y 1450 px, inside 90 px side margins. One to three words at a time.

Platform UI · bottom

The bottom 320 px is covered by TikTok, Reels and Shorts buttons. No text, logos or key action there.

Universal safe area: 900 × 1400 px centred. Headline tags sit at the very top (y ≈ 250 px) when no widget is showing.

13 · On-screen graphics

The graphics system

Topic widget, image card. Header: fin + topic in cyan + "ARKS SHARKS". Image with an ILLUSTRATION tag when AI-made. Headline in two lines. Source line, always.
Stat card. One big number, a progress bar, a two-line headline and the source.
Ratio card. "N in M" with icons; the highlighted one in Shark Cyan.
Headline tag + captions.
End card. Blurred studio, logo, episode line.

Widgets

Change on every line of dialogue. Slide in 36 px and fade in over 0.3 s; fade out over 0.2 s.

Sources

Every factual card shows "Source: …" with the outlet and date. Opinions and jokes don't need one.

Name tags

White on Deep Navy, under each host for the first 4 seconds of a video.

Logo slam

At the title moment: logo scales from 150% to 100% with a bass hit, holds about 2 s.

14 · Motion & editing

Hook fast, then talk like friends

Opener 0–4 s: holographic circuit shark around the Burj Khalifa.
4–6 s: it dives through the studio glass.
6–9.7 s: lights up, logo lands, sonic logo.

Editing rules

  • Hook in the first 3 s, with on-screen text from frame 1.
  • Trailers: a new cut every 2–4 s. Episodes: let conversations breathe.
  • Every episode starts with the fixed opener (9.7 s).
  • Hard cuts between shots; no flashy transitions.
  • Captions on everything: most viewers watch muted.
  • Cover AI glitches (outfit morphs, odd hands) with a reaction cutaway.

Approved trailer structure (55 s)

Hook · 7 s
Title · logo slam · 5 s
Who we are · 7.5 s
Rapid-fire news · 9 s
Funny corner · 14 s
Sign-off · 9 s
End card

15 · Sound identity

Their real voices, clean and close

Voices

Always the hosts' own cloned voices (Cartesia), UK English with their natural accents. Never a stock voice.

Lip-sync route: the animation is generated from the lines, then each speaker is converted back to his real voice so the timing matches the mouth.

Voice processing

  • De-reverb (WPE)
  • Neural noise removal (RNNoise, voice model)
  • Gentle spectral denoise
  • De-click, de-ess
  • EQ: −2.5 dB at 220 Hz, +2.2 dB at 3.8 kHz, +1.5 dB air shelf
  • Light compression (2.2:1)

No noise gate: it swallows soft consonants.

Loudness

Master−14 LUFS integrated
True peak≤ −1.5 dBTP
Music bed18–20 dB under dialogue, side-chain ducked
SFX6 dB under dialogue

Sonic logo

Low drone → riser → sub impact, then a bright three-note motif A – E – A with a shimmer tail. Plays with the logo at the end of the opener and on title slams.

Music

Original, licence-free beds made for the show: 120 BPM cinematic electronic, chords Am – F – C – G. Trailer mode has the hook drone, riser and impacts; conversation mode is a soft groove. Never use tracks with unclear rights.

16 · Tone of voice & content rules

Sound like two mates who know their stuff

Voice

  • UK English, short sentences, spoken not written.
  • Light Arabic flavour used naturally: Yalla, Habibi, Wallah, Shukran.
  • Questions and reactions between the hosts keep it a conversation.
  • Explain jargon in one breath ("agentic AI: AI that actually does tasks for you").

Content rules

  • Every fact is real, recent and sourced; the source appears on screen.
  • AI-made pictures carry the "ILLUSTRATION" tag.
  • Funny corner: real, documented AI fails only.
  • Kind humour: laugh at the tech, never at people.
  • No claims about real people's experiences unless they're true.

Say it like this

✓ “Mate, you already can! Umm Suqeim and Jumeirah, since March.”

✕ “Commercial autonomous mobility services have been deployed in select districts.”

✓ “Treat AI like a brilliant intern: fast, helpful, but check its work.”

✕ “AI will replace everyone by next year.” (unsourced hype)

Sign-off & catchphrases

“We hunt the AI news… so you don't have to.” · “New features. Funny fails. Real talk.” · “ARKS Sharks! Yalla… dive in!”
